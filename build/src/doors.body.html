Compression · how much does a grade point still distinguish near the top of the scale?

Same school, different doors

Every California high school sends applicants to all nine undergraduate campuses. The students who apply to Merced are, on the whole, further down their own class than the students who apply to Berkeley. Comparing those slices inside one school in one year holds the school constant: whatever its teaching, its neighborhood and its actual achievement, they are the same for both slices. Whatever separates the slices is the reach of the pool, not the school.

The slices also start at different distances from the top of the scale, and the scale has a hard cap: the closer a pool already sits to it, the less room any rise has, and the less one grade point can still distinguish. This figure measures how far each slice moved against the room it had left. Compression of that kind is a loss of resolution whatever produced it — easier grading, heavier course weighting, or real improvement running out of headroom.
